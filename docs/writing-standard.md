# Writing standard

These rules apply to every lesson page. They came out of the Module 1 prose review,
where readers got lost not because the sentences were weak but because the words sat
in the wrong places.

1. **One name per thing.** Pick the term once and keep it. A page is *saved*, a site
   *answers* or *doesn't answer*, a *match* is a game on the schedule, a *result* is
   its score, and a result that went into the table is *counted*. Use *verified* in
   prose. Prove is only the name of Touchline's step.
2. **One new term at a time.** Give each new term a plain sentence and a Friday
   example before the next term arrives.
3. **One transition per boundary.** The NextPart line does that job. End a part on an
   observation or an open question, not on a preview of the next section.
4. **Exact, not absolute.** Say what a mechanism guarantees and what it does not.
   Reading both schools' pages gives two chances to find a result. It does not
   promise one.
5. **Labels that carry the lesson are full sentences.** Controls and legends stay
   short.

Case headings name the subject ("The expected set"). The beat word (Look, Name it,
What Touchline does, Your claim) sits above the heading as a small label.
